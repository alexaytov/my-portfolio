On 6 July 2026, I presented **Knowledge Before Action: An Architecture for Autonomous AI Agents in Information Systems** at Mathematics Days in Sofia, in the section **Mathematical Foundations of Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence**.

I am a PhD candidate at the **Institute of Mathematics and Informatics, Bulgarian Academy of Sciences (IMI-BAS)**, with a research focus on integrating AI into real-world systems. This talk introduced an architectural approach to a central question: what does an AI agent need to know before it can act reliably?

## From domain knowledge to action

The presentation examines how fragmented information systems and missing domain context can lead agents to choose the wrong procedures or act on incomplete information. It proposes a five-layer architecture connecting data sources, a knowledge graph, retrieval-augmented generation, an agent engine, and the application interface.

The guiding principle is **knowledge before action**: the agent should consult relevant, authoritative domain knowledge before interacting with an external system. The proposed framework separates the responsibilities of each layer and includes access control, auditability, and human oversight as architectural concerns.

Bulgarian citizen-service delivery provides an illustrative setting, with eligibility rules, required documents, and service procedures. The slides also describe a **planned evaluation** comparing the full system with variants that omit the knowledge graph, retrieval, or both, using task completion and form-filling accuracy as measures.
